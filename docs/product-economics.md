## Rising Son product-fit gates
A commercially attractive quote does not override product quality. A candidate must first be evaluated against the intended experience:
- rich/bright aroma
- strong, full flavor without harsh/burnt character
- smooth, earthy direction
- low perceived acidity
- low bitterness
- pleasant/clean finish

Decaf should resemble the Original experience where quality permits. If forcing similarity produces an inferior decaf, choose quality instead. Record decaffeination method when available, but do not select on process name alone.

## True delivered economics
For each product/variant and order quantity Q, capture:
- net coffee weight and retail price
- retail $/oz = retail price / net ounces
- supplier coffee cost
- bag/label/packaging cost
- grinding surcharge where applicable
- pick/pack cost
- outbound shipping cost
- setup/order fees allocated per order
- payment-processing cost
- shipping charged to customer
- discounts
- replacement/refund allowance once evidence supports one

Then calculate:
- gross revenue = merchandise + shipping charged - discounts
- variable product cost = unit landed product cost × Q
- shipping subsidy = fulfillment/carrier shipping cost - shipping charged
- contribution = gross revenue - product cost - processor fee - shipping subsidy - other variable order costs
- contribution margin % = contribution / gross revenue
- delivered customer cost = merchandise + customer-paid shipping - discounts
- delivered customer $/oz = delivered customer cost / total coffee ounces

Do not hard-code a processor fee until the applicable live account rate is verified.

## Value-first optimization
Rising Son's working positioning is "more good coffee for your money," not luxury-for-luxury's-sake or lowest-price coffee.

Optimization order:
1. Clear the minimum product-quality bar.
2. Confirm professional/direct fulfillment is operationally viable.
3. Compare true delivered economics.
4. Maximize useful coffee quantity/value for the customer while maintaining sustainable contribution.
5. Prefer simpler variants and operations when economic differences are immaterial.

Bag size is therefore an optimization variable, not a predetermined standard.

## Subscription economics
A subscription must provide a real customer benefit rather than a cosmetic "subscribe & save" label. For each candidate cadence calculate:
- one-time delivered $/oz
- subscription delivered $/oz
- customer savings per shipment and %
- Rising Son contribution per shipment and %
- expected shipping/fulfillment savings, if any
- break-even discount
- effect of quantity/multi-bag shipments
- failed-payment/refund/skip/cancellation costs when evidence exists

Do not publish a discount, savings percentage or cadence until these economics are verified.

## Supplier comparison
Do not collapse supplier selection into price alone. Compare each supplier on verified dimensions:
- sensory/product fit
- Original/Decaf fit and consistency
- available net weights
- whole bean/ground support and surcharges
- MOQ flexibility
- direct/private-label fulfillment capability
- true delivered cost
- customer delivered $/oz at viable margin
- production/ship lead time
- tracking and exception handling
- replacement/loss policy
- subscription/repeat-order compatibility
- evidence quality and responsiveness

Record facts rather than inventing a numeric supplier score before samples and commercial evidence exist.

## Founding 40 acquisition economics
The Founding 40 tumbler is modeled separately from the normal coffee retail price.

Principle: a Founding 40 customer should pay the same normal launch coffee price they would otherwise pay. Do not increase that price specifically to recover the tumbler. If confirmed, the tumbler is Rising Son's gift at $0/no additional charge and its cost is absorbed from company contribution as a launch/customer-acquisition expense.

Track:
- normal coffee revenue and normal order contribution before Founding benefit;
- tumbler base unit cost;
- customization/engraving/printing cost;
- individual numbering cost;
- inbound freight allocated per tumbler;
- incremental fulfillment/pick-pack cost;
- incremental outbound shipping caused by tumbler;
- breakage/replacement allowance;
- total tumbler landed-and-delivered cost per activated customer;
- total program cost for 40 activated customers plus any approved spares;
- contribution after Founding benefit;
- first-order acquisition cost;
- repeat purchase/subscription contribution and eventual customer lifetime value when evidence exists.

Calculations:
- Founding acquisition expense per customer = all incremental tumbler + customization + fulfillment + shipping costs attributable to the gift
- Founding first-order contribution after gift = normal order contribution - Founding acquisition expense
- total Founding launch expense = actual activated Founding customers × acquisition expense, plus approved sample/spare/setup costs

The first order does not have to recover the entire acquisition expense if approved economics support acquiring a repeat customer, but this must be an explicit business decision rather than hidden by raising the Founding customer's coffee price.

### Affordability scenarios when quotes arrive
Model at minimum:
- 1 bag + 1 tumbler;
- 2 bags + 1 tumbler;
- 3 bags + 1 tumbler;
- Original and Decaf variants;
- customer-paid and subsidized shipping cases;
- 40 fulfilled tumblers plus a prudent spare/replacement quantity;
- contribution before and after the Founding benefit;
- repeat-order and subscription break-even needed to recover acquisition expense.

Until real quotes exist, every tumbler cost remains UNKNOWN and the public offer remains a plan rather than a guarantee.
