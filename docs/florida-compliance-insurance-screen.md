## What official sources establish

- FDACS says retail food establishment permits cover businesses selling directly to customers, including businesses selling prepackaged foods. The permit process carries applicable fees and annual renewal. This is not a determination that a brand with no Florida physical food establishment needs its own permit; ask FDACS to classify this exact arrangement in writing. Source: https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food-Establishments/Retail-Food-Establishment-Permit
- FDACS lists FoodSafety@FDACS.gov and 1-800-HELP-FLA (435-7652) for assistance and says permit fees depend on food activities. The $55.10 shown on the retail page is the initial *optional voluntary plan review* charge, not a general permit fee. Source: same FDACS page.
- FDA food facility registration applies to facilities manufacturing, processing, packing or holding food; a brand office that performs none of those functions should not be assumed to be the food facility. Verify the supplier's actual facilities and registration/permit status. Source: https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-regulation-food-and-dietary-supplements/registration-food-facilities-and-other-submissions
- Florida's official workers' compensation guidance requires coverage for nonconstruction employers with four or more employees, counting certain owners. A one-person, nonconstruction launch below that threshold is not shown as mandatorily covered by that rule, subject to entity/worker classification. Source: https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/coverage-requirements
- The FDACS retail permit guidance does not list a blanket requirement to buy general or product liability insurance. That absence is not a definitive legal finding for every selling channel or contract. Supplier, marketplace, landlord, lender or event terms can require coverage, and Rising Son can still face claims even if the supplier has insurance.
